# Team Charters: Empowered vs Feature Teams

The conflict, the decision criteria, the transition politics, and worked examples behind the empowered-teams skill.

## 1. The Conflict (Marty Cagan, SVPG)

- **Empowered product teams:** cross-functional (product, design, engineering), given a small number of significant problems to solve, owning the solution, accountable for business results. Cagan: feature teams treat engineering as a cost center to be fed requirements — empowered teams treat it as the talent that discovers the solution.
- **Feature teams:** delivery-focused, aligned by output, given features to ship, accountable for delivery dates. They exist because organizations want predictability, roadmap control, and sales promises fulfilled.
- The disagreement is not about talent; it is about what the organization is willing to hand over. The decision criteria below decide.

## 2. Decision Criteria

**Choose empowered teams when:**
- Product-market fit is established, or is being searched for in a dynamic market where the solution is unknown.
- The team has high product/design/engineering skill, or coaching is funded to build it.
- Leadership will accept solutions it did not order and commit to problems in writing.

**Choose feature teams when:**
- Requirements are dictated by signed contracts, regulatory deadlines, or an execution-only business model (custom enterprise builds, compliance rollouts).
- Delivery predictability is the business model itself (some agency and ops models).
- There is a skills gap with no coaching available — then build skills first, or charter honestly as delivery.

## 3. When Feature Teams Are Legitimate (worked shapes)

- **Regulatory/contractual (hypothetical):** a bank payments team shipping a regulator-mandated reporting format by a statutory date. The "what" and "when" are externally dictated; solution autonomy is narrow. Charter it honestly: delivery accountability, dates as commitments, no empowerment language.
- **Execution-only business model:** custom enterprise builds where the client writes the spec and pays for the spec.
- **Important:** legitimacy is about the work, not the org chart. A regulated firm can run empowered teams on the non-dictated slice and feature teams on the dictated slice. Mixed charters are fine; mixed labels are not.

## 4. Transition Politics & Pilot Teams

- Transitions fail on politics, not frameworks: middle managers lose "their" roadmap, sales has promised features, execs want control. SVPG's pilot-team guidance: start with one volunteer team and one visible problem, let results argue, and scale only what passed its checkpoint.
- Give the pilot real authority: a budget of customer time, telemetry access, and a manager who coaches instead of assigning.
- Expect the first checkpoint to test leadership, not the team: the team will propose something leadership did not order. If that is overruled without a constraint reason (legal, viability, feasibility), the transition is over — say so plainly and charter honestly.

## 5. Worked Examples

- **Empowered (real):** Cagan's canonical deployments cite companies such as Amazon and Netflix; the publicly documented shape many teams borrow is Spotify's squad model — squads owned outcomes (e.g. making discovery effortless), held product/design/engineering skill, and were measured on usage outcomes rather than releases. Borrow the charter shape, not the org-chart cargo cult.
- **Feature (honest, hypothetical):** a 12-person team at an insurance company shipping a regulator-mandated claims-format change by a statutory date. Feature charter, dated, delivery-accountable — and no shame attached.

## 6. Charter Anatomy (both modes)

Every charter, empowered or feature, states: what the team owns, what it is accountable for, its authority, its constraints, its evidence access, and its review question.
